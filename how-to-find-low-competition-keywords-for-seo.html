How to Find Low Competition Keywords for SEO: Complete Guide

Published: November 25, 2026
Read Time: 15 min read
Author: Balamurugan P

Ranking on Google is becoming increasingly competitive. Many websites target the same popular keywords, making it difficult for new websites and small businesses to achieve first page rankings.

One effective way to improve your SEO strategy is to target low competition keywords. These are search queries where you may have a realistic opportunity to compete by publishing relevant, useful, and well-optimized content.

Instead of targeting only broad keywords with high search volume, you can identify specific search queries that match your audience's needs and have fewer strong competing pages.

In this guide, you will learn how to find low competition keywords for SEO using keyword research tools, Google search results, competitor analysis, and practical keyword research techniques.

What Are Low Competition Keywords?

Low competition keywords are search terms where the existing search results offer a realistic opportunity for a website to rank on Google's first page.

These keywords typically exhibit more specific search intent, fewer highly authoritative competing domains, or weaker existing content in the search engine result pages (SERPs).

Consider the progression below:

  • Broad keyword: SEO
  • More specific keyword: Technical SEO audit
  • Long-tail keyword: Technical SEO audit checklist for small business websites

The broader keyword is extremely difficult to rank for because thousands of established enterprise domains target it with decades of accumulated backlinks. Conversely, specific search queries offer immediate opportunities to produce comprehensive answers that directly satisfy user intent.

Keyword Type Example Query Search Volume Ranking Difficulty Conversion Intent
Broad Term (Head) SEO Very High Extremely High Vague / Informational
Specific Keyword (Body) Technical SEO audit Moderate Medium Targeted Informational
Long-Tail Keyword Technical SEO audit checklist for small business Focused / Niche Low to Moderate High Commercial / Actionable

However, low competition does not mean zero effort. You must always assess search intent, content depth, domain authority, and the real-time Google search landscape before committing your resources.

Why Should You Target Low Competition Keywords?

Targeting low difficulty keywords is the fastest path to organic growth, especially for new websites, blogs, and local service providers. Discover the primary strategic benefits below:

1. Better Ranking Opportunities

Highly competitive search terms are dominated by major media publications, Fortune 500 corporations, and veteran industry blogs. Targeting niche queries allows your content to rank on page one much faster without needing hundreds of premium backlinks.

2. Reach a More Relevant Audience

Specific keywords expose precise user needs. Someone searching for "how to fix website indexing issues" possesses an acute technical problem requiring immediate step-by-step guidance. Creating dedicated content around that exact intent attracts high-value readers who stay longer and convert readily.

3. Improve Organic Traffic

A portfolio of 20 to 50 low competition keywords generating 50 to 300 monthly visits each builds predictable, diversified organic traffic. You avoid relying on a single volatile head term that could lose position overnight during an algorithm update.

4. Generate Relevant Business Enquiries

Low competition keywords frequently possess strong commercial purchase intent. Examples include:

  • Technical SEO audit services
  • Local SEO services for small businesses
  • Ecommerce SEO consultant
  • Website SEO audit company

While search volume for these phrases is modest, every visitor is an active prospective buyer looking for specialized professional solutions.

5. Build Topical Authority

Publishing clusters of detailed articles across subtopics demonstrates domain mastery to search engines. For instance, an SEO blog can publish interrelated guides on:

  • Technical SEO audits
  • XML sitemaps & Robots.txt
  • Canonical tags & duplicate content
  • Website indexing & crawl budget
  • Core Web Vitals & JavaScript SEO

Connecting these posts via strategic internal links builds an authoritative semantic hub that gradually lifts rankings across your entire domain.

How to Find Low Competition Keywords for SEO: 10 Practical Methods

Follow these 10 actionable methods to systematically uncover high-potential keyword opportunities.

1. Start With Your Main Topic

Begin your research by identifying the core pillars of your website or business offerings. These foundational phrases are called seed keywords.

For an SEO and digital marketing specialist, initial seed terms include:

  • SEO services
  • Technical SEO
  • Local SEO
  • Keyword research
  • Website optimization
  • Link building

Branch out from these seeds to create specialized topic variations. For example, expanding the seed "technical SEO" generates:

  • Technical SEO audit checklist
  • Technical SEO for WordPress
  • Technical SEO issues
  • Technical SEO tools
  • Technical SEO for ecommerce websites
  • How to improve website crawling

Never pick keywords solely on search volume; always assess whether they align with your business goals and service competencies.

2. Use Google Autocomplete

Google Autocomplete is one of the fastest, completely free keyword research utilities available. When you start typing into Google's search box, algorithms suggest predictive queries based on real user search activity, regional relevance, and trending topics.

Step-by-step Autocomplete workflow:

  1. Open Google Search in an Incognito window to avoid personalized history bias.
  2. Type your seed keyword (e.g., "technical SEO").
  3. Review the drop-down recommendations.
  4. Add wildcard prefixes and prepositions before or after your term, such as:
    • "Technical SEO for..." (e.g., Shopify, beginners, SaaS)
    • "How to improve..."
    • "Best tools for..."
    • "How to find..."
    • "SEO checklist for..."
  5. Log all viable suggestions into your keyword tracking spreadsheet.

Autocomplete surfaces genuine user queries that searchers type every day, highlighting valuable long-tail variations.

3. Find Keyword Ideas Using Google Related Searches

Google displays related searches at the bottom of the SERP and interactive questions in the People Also Ask (PAA) box. These features provide direct access to Google's semantic query associations.

For example, searching for "keyword research" surfaces related topics such as:

  • Keyword research tools
  • Keyword difficulty
  • Long-tail keywords
  • Keyword research strategy
  • Competitor keyword analysis

Additionally, review the People Also Ask box for real customer inquiries:

  • What are low competition keywords?
  • How do I find easy keywords to rank for?
  • Which keyword research tools are free?
  • What is keyword difficulty in SEO?

Incorporate these questions directly as H2 or H3 subheadings within your guides, or write dedicated companion articles when a query reflects standalone search intent.

4. Use Google Keyword Planner

Google Keyword Planner is a free tool within Google Ads that reveals estimated search volumes, historical search trends, and related keyword variations.

How to extract SEO keywords with Keyword Planner:

  1. Sign in to your Google Ads account and navigate to Tools > Keyword Planner.
  2. Click Discover new keywords.
  3. Enter your seed keywords or enter a competitor's domain URL to scan their page content.
  4. Configure your target geographical location and language.
  5. Filter the results to exclude adult ideas and export the list to CSV.

Crucial Note: The "Competition" column in Keyword Planner reflects paid Google Ads bidding competition among advertisers—not organic SEO ranking difficulty. A keyword marked "Low Competition" in Google Ads can still be fiercely competitive organically. Use Keyword Planner to gauge search interest and discover variations, then evaluate organic difficulty independently.

5. Use Ahrefs to Find Low Difficulty Keywords

Ahrefs Keyword Explorer allows you to filter billions of search queries across major global search engines with precise metrics.

Finding low difficulty targets in Ahrefs:

  1. Open Keyword Explorer and enter your seed terms.
  2. Navigate to the Matching terms or Related terms report.
  3. Set the KD (Keyword Difficulty) filter to maximum 15 or 20 (representing low backlink barrier).
  4. Apply a minimum volume filter (e.g., 50 to 500 monthly searches).
  5. Inspect the top 10 SERP overview to check the Domain Rating (DR) of current ranking URLs.

Because Ahrefs KD relies heavily on referring domain counts, always examine the SERP manually to see if ranking pages have weak content that you can surpass.

6. Find Low Competition Keywords Using Semrush

Semrush provides the Keyword Magic Tool, which organizes keyword databases into convenient subtopic groups.

Steps to find low competition terms in Semrush:

  1. Enter your seed topic into the Keyword Magic Tool.
  2. Filter by Keyword Difficulty (KD%): select Very Easy (0-14%) or Easy (15-29%).
  3. Filter by Search Intent (Informational, Commercial, or Transactional) depending on whether you are writing a blog or a service landing page.
  4. Review the question tab to isolate high-intent instructional questions.
  5. Export your prioritized short-list for manual SERP verification.

7. Analyze Competitor Keywords

Analyzing competitors reveals existing keyword gaps where other sites in your industry receive steady organic traffic from topics you have yet to cover.

Competitor analysis workflow:

  1. Identify 3 to 5 direct competitors or mid-tier niche blogs in your space.
  2. Paste their domain into Ahrefs Site Explorer or Semrush Domain Overview.
  3. Open the Organic Keywords report.
  4. Filter for keywords ranking in positions 1 to 20 with moderate difficulty.
  5. Look for content gaps—topics they cover poorly or have neglected to update.

If a competitor ranks on page one with a brief 600-word post from 2022, creating a comprehensive, up-to-date guide with modern examples and custom graphics gives you a strong chance to capture that traffic.

8. Target Long Tail Keywords

Long-tail keywords are specific search queries that typically consist of 3 to 6 words. They account for the vast majority of all web searches and offer superior conversion rates because they reflect specific purchase intent.

Broad Query Targeted Long-Tail Opportunity Searcher Intent
SEO SEO checklist for small business websites Practical implementation guide for entrepreneurs
Link building Link building strategies for new websites White-hat tactics for low-authority domains
Keyword research How to find low competition keywords for SEO Actionable step-by-step tutorial
Local SEO Local SEO for dental clinics Industry-specific commercial service
Website speed How to improve website speed on WordPress Platform-specific technical troubleshooting

While long-tail keywords generally have lower individual search volume, aggregate traffic from dozens of long-tail articles adds up significantly while delivering qualified leads.

9. Find Keywords From Online Communities and Discussions

Forums and discussion boards represent unfiltered customer questions. They are ideal for uncovering emerging topics before keyword tools even register search volume.

Top community sources to monitor:

  • Reddit: Subreddits like r/SEO, r/digitalmarketing, r/smallbusiness, and r/webdev.
  • Quora: High-view questions with outdated or brief answers.
  • LinkedIn Groups & Polls: Industry-specific professional debates.
  • Customer Inquiries: The real questions sent to your support email or consultation forms.

Common real-world questions frequently translate directly into high-ranking articles:

  • Why are my website pages not indexed in Google?
  • Why did my Google rankings suddenly drop after a redesign?
  • How can I improve Core Web Vitals without a developer?
  • Why is my website not ranking for its target keywords?

10. Use Google Search Console to Find Hidden Keyword Opportunities

Google Search Console is your most reliable first-party data source. It reveals the exact queries where your pages are already gaining impressions in search results.

How to uncover hidden gems in Search Console:

  1. Log in to Search Console and select your domain property.
  2. Go to Performance > Search Results.
  3. Set the timeframe to the last 3 months.
  4. Check Total impressions and Average position checkboxes.
  5. Switch to the Queries tab and filter by position greater than 10 (pages ranking on pages 2 to 4).
  6. Identify queries with hundreds of impressions but very few clicks.
Search Query Impressions Clicks Average Position Strategic Action
Technical SEO checklist 450 12 18.4 Expand technical section & add schema markup
Website indexing guide 320 8 16.2 Add dedicated H2 subheading answering indexing errors
Canonical tags SEO 210 5 22.1 Create dedicated companion guide with code samples
Robots.txt best practices 180 4 19.7 Add downloadable robots.txt template & internal links

These striking queries prove that Google already considers your domain relevant. By expanding existing sections, clarifying subheadings, or writing dedicated companion articles, you can elevate these terms into the top 5 positions.

How to Evaluate Keyword Competition Before Selecting a Keyword

Discovering keyword ideas is only half the battle. You must systematically evaluate whether your website can realistically rank on page one. Assess these five critical factors:

1. Keyword Difficulty (KD)

Keyword difficulty scores in Ahrefs, Semrush, or Moz serve as an initial filter. However, remember that every tool uses its own proprietary algorithm—some weigh backlinks exclusively, while others incorporate search intent and SERP features. Use KD as an initial benchmark, never as an absolute verdict.

2. Search Volume

Monthly search volume estimates how many times users search for a phrase. While high volume is enticing, a low-volume query (e.g., 80 to 200 monthly searches) targeting a specific service can generate higher conversion rates than a broad keyword receiving 10,000 ambiguous searches.

3. Search Intent

Google prioritizes content that satisfies the user's underlying search purpose. The four fundamental types of search intent include:

  • Informational: Users seeking knowledge, guides, or definitions (e.g., "what is schema markup").
  • Navigational: Users looking for a specific brand or URL (e.g., "Google Search Console login").
  • Commercial Investigation: Users comparing options or vetting providers (e.g., "best SEO tools for small business").
  • Transactional: Users ready to hire or purchase (e.g., "hire SEO consultant Chennai").

Always examine the current top 10 search results to verify whether Google favors comprehensive blog posts, service landing pages, videos, or tools.

4. Competitor Website Strength

Analyze the domains currently holding page one positions:

  • Are they major national brands (Forbes, HubSpot, Wikipedia) or independent blogs?
  • What is their average Domain Rating / Domain Authority?
  • How many unique referring domains point to the specific ranking URL?
  • Is their content thorough, well-formatted, and up to date, or is it thin and outdated?

If page one is populated with low-authority sites, community forums (Reddit, Quora), or obsolete articles, that keyword represents a prime ranking opportunity.

5. SERP Analysis

Always inspect the live Google search results page manually. Note the presence of rich features:

  • AI Overviews: Does Google synthesize an AI response at the top? (Optimize with clear definition sentences and structured lists for AEO).
  • Featured Snippets: Can you win position zero with a concise table or bulleted summary?
  • People Also Ask boxes: What related questions should you address?
  • Video & Image Packs: Does the query demand visual explanations or infographics?

Core rules for keyword selection:

  • Prioritize queries directly relevant to your core service offerings.
  • Target keywords where user search intent is clear and unambiguous.
  • Fill existing content gaps that competitors have overlooked.
  • Avoid creating duplicate pages targeting near-identical intent to prevent keyword cannibalization.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Finding Low Competition Keywords

Even seasoned marketers stumble when executing keyword research. Avoid these common traps:

  1. Relying blindly on tool difficulty scores: SEO tool metrics are estimates based primarily on backlinks. Always inspect the live Google SERP to see who is actually ranking.
  2. Ignoring search intent: If searchers want a fast calculation tool and you provide a 3,000-word essay, your page will struggle to retain users regardless of difficulty.
  3. Targeting zero-relevance keywords: Driving traffic from irrelevant keywords that never convert into newsletter subscribers or clients wastes valuable crawling resources.
  4. Neglecting competitor content quality: If the top results already feature comprehensive, original research, you cannot outrank them with a generic summary.
  5. Obsessing over high search volume: 100 targeted visitors seeking your specific service provide far greater ROI than 5,000 casual readers skimming a broad definition.
  6. Creating duplicate articles (Keyword Cannibalization): Publishing multiple posts on "low competition keywords" and "easy SEO keywords" forces your own pages to compete against each other.
  7. Publishing without strategic internal links: Failing to link new articles to relevant cornerstone guides limits page authority transfer and damages crawlability.
  8. Failing to update your keyword strategy: Competitor landscapes and search trends evolve. Re-audit your keyword targeting every quarter to protect your rankings.

Frequently Asked Questions About Low Competition Keywords

1. What are low competition keywords in SEO?

Low competition keywords are search queries where a website may have a realistic opportunity to compete in Google search results. Their difficulty depends on the ranking pages, website strength, content quality, search intent, and competition.

2. How can I find low competition keywords for free?

You can use Google Autocomplete, Google related searches, People Also Ask, Google Keyword Planner, Google Search Console, and Google Trends to generate and evaluate keyword ideas without paid software.

3. What is a good keyword difficulty score?

There is no universal keyword difficulty score that guarantees a keyword is easy to rank for. Different SEO tools use different calculations. Review the actual Google search results page (SERP) and compare them with your website's authority and content capabilities.

4. Are long-tail keywords easier to rank for?

Long-tail keywords can provide more specific ranking opportunities because they often have clearer search intent. However, some long-tail keywords are still highly competitive, so evaluate each query individually.

5. Can a new website rank for low competition keywords?

Yes. A new website can compete for relevant keywords by publishing useful content, matching search intent, building a logical internal linking structure, and maintaining good technical SEO. Rankings are not guaranteed and develop as topical authority grows.

6. How many keywords should I target in one blog?

Start with one primary keyword and a group of closely related secondary keywords. The number depends on the topic depth and search intent. Avoid forcing unrelated keywords into one article.

7. Is Google Keyword Planner useful for SEO keyword research?

Yes. Google Keyword Planner is useful for discovering keyword ideas and estimating search demand. However, its competition metric represents advertising competition, not organic SEO difficulty.

8. How often should I perform keyword research?

Review keyword opportunities regularly, especially when planning new content, updating existing articles, or evaluating changes in search performance. A quarterly review is a useful starting point for many websites.

Key Takeaways

Targeting low competition keywords is the most reliable strategy for building organic search visibility, high-intent traffic, and sustainable topical authority.

  • Target Search Intent Directly: Align your content structure precisely with what users expect—whether actionable tutorials, comparison tables, or service solutions.
  • Combine Free & Paid Intelligence: Pair real-time Google Autocomplete and Search Console query data with Ahrefs or Semrush keyword difficulty filters.
  • Always Inspect the Real SERP: Never trust automated difficulty metrics blindly. Look for weak, outdated competitor content and unaddressed customer questions.
  • Build Authoritative Clusters: Connect related long-tail articles to your pillar guides using descriptive, contextual internal links.

Final Thoughts

Finding low competition keywords is an indispensable pillar of a practical, results-driven SEO strategy.

Instead of chasing only popular head terms with intense competition, focus on specific search queries that match your audience's immediate needs and offer realistic opportunities to rank on page one.

Start with Google Autocomplete, related searches, Google Keyword Planner, and Google Search Console. Layer on Ahrefs or Semrush for competitive gap insights, and always verify the live Google search results page before finalizing your targets.

A rigorous keyword research process, backed by helpful content, strategic internal linking, and sound technical SEO, will steadily expand your organic search reach and drive qualified business inquiries over time.
